01 / INTRODUCTION

What is preventive maintenance?

Preventive maintenance is the regular care performed before a fault occurs to keep a laptop clean, cool, reliable, and ready to use.

WHAT CAN GO WRONG?

Dust, heat, and daily wear build up over time.

Dust can collect on the screen, keyboard, ports, fan, and heat sink. Blocked airflow can make a laptop noisy or hot, while spills, rough handling, and poor storage can damage its components.

THE PREVENTIVE APPROACH

Inspect, clean, and protect on a routine.

Clean accessible surfaces regularly, keep vents clear, protect the laptop from liquids and heat, and arrange internal cleaning when needed. Small, safe habits can reduce avoidable wear.

⚠ External cleaning is suitable for routine care. Open the case only when the manufacturer permits it and you have the right skills; otherwise, use an authorized technician.

02 / EXTERNAL MAINTENANCE

Care for the outside first

External maintenance removes everyday dirt without opening the laptop. A regular, gentle routine keeps the screen, keyboard, casing, ports, and air vents in better condition.

01

FIRST

Shut down and prepare a clean workspace

Save your work, shut down the laptop completely, unplug the charger, and disconnect accessories. Let the laptop cool. Place it on a stable, clean surface and wash and dry your hands.

02

THEN

Clean the screen and outer casing

Use a clean, dry microfiber cloth for fingerprints and dust. If the manufacturer allows a screen-safe cleaner, apply a small amount to the cloth, never directly to the screen. Wipe gently and keep moisture away from seams and openings.

03

AFTER THAT

Clean the keyboard, ports, and air vents

Turn the laptop upside down only if its manual recommends it. Use a soft brush or dry cloth on the keyboard and exterior of the vents. Do not push debris into ports, insert objects, or spray liquid into any opening.

04

FINALLY

Inspect the accessories and return to use

Check the charger cable, plugs, hinges, and casing for wear or damage. Let all surfaces dry before reconnecting power. Keep the laptop on a hard surface so its vents remain unobstructed.

03 / INTERNAL MAINTENANCE

Clean inside only when it is safe

Internal cleaning focuses on dust around the cooling fan and heat sink. It is less frequent than surface cleaning and should follow the laptop's service manual and warranty terms.

!

Safety first: If the battery is swollen, damaged, hot, or leaking, do not open or press on the laptop. Stop using it and contact a qualified repair professional. Never puncture or remove a built-in battery yourself.

01

FIRST

Check the service manual and warranty

Confirm that user access is allowed for your model. Back up important files, shut down the laptop, unplug the charger, and remove only accessories or batteries designed to be user-removable. If opening the case may void the warranty, book authorized service.

02

THEN

Prepare for electrostatic safety

Work on a clear, non-carpeted surface and use the anti-static precautions specified by the manufacturer. Use the correct screwdriver and keep screws organized. After opening the case, disconnect the internal battery only as the service manual directs.

03

AFTER THAT

Remove dust from the cooling system

Use manufacturer-approved tools, such as a soft anti-static brush or short bursts of electronics-safe compressed air. Hold the fan still so it does not spin freely, and keep the air can upright. Do not touch the motherboard, force connectors, use a household vacuum, or remove the heat sink or thermal paste unnecessarily.

04

FINALLY

Reassemble and check normal operation

Reconnect the battery and internal cables as instructed, then reinstall the cover and screws without overtightening. Check that nothing is trapped or loose. Power on the laptop and listen for unusual fan noise; if it runs hot or behaves unexpectedly, shut it down and seek professional service.

04 / ROUTINE MAINTENANCE PLAN

Build simple habits

Maintenance frequency depends on the laptop, its environment, and the manufacturer's recommendations. Dusty homes, pets, and heavy daily use may require more frequent inspection.

REGULARLY

Protect during everyday use

Use the laptop on a hard, flat surface; keep drinks away; carry it in a padded case; and avoid blocking its cooling vents.

AS NEEDED

Wipe exterior surfaces

When dust or fingerprints appear, shut down and gently clean the screen, keyboard, and casing with suitable materials.

PERIODICALLY

Inspect vents and performance

Check for blocked vents, unusual fan noise, excess heat, or unexpected slowdowns. Follow the service interval in the user manual.

WHEN NEEDED

Request internal service

Ask a qualified technician to clean inside when dust buildup or cooling problems cannot be safely addressed from the outside.

06 / GRAMMAR IN CONTEXT

Past modals

Past modals connect the grammar lesson to laptop care. Use should have for a past action that was advisable, and could have for a possible past cause.

SHOULD HAVE · PAST ADVICE

We should have inspected the air vents regularly.

Regular inspections would have been a sensible preventive step.
COULD HAVE · PAST POSSIBILITY

Dust could have collected around the cooling fan.

This describes one possible result of not cleaning the cooling system.
